## The main rule: every video is a story
Even a news explainer has a person, a moment and a turn in it.
- **A person:** usually you ("I"), sometimes the viewer ("you"), sometimes a real person in the news.
- **A moment:** a time, a place, an object. "It's 11 PM, I have 23 tabs open, and one of them is a pricing page I'm reading like a thriller."
- **A turn:** the point where what you expected and what happened split. The key fact lands here.
- **A payoff:** the ending calls back to the opening moment, so the video closes the loop it opened.

Tell stories in the present tense ("So I open the app, and the first thing it asks me is..."). Mix sentence
lengths: a few short ones, one longer one, then a line of two words. Let one line stand alone as a pause.

## Hooks: relatable first, clever second
The first line should make someone think "that's me". Put an "I" or a "you" and one concrete thing in the
first five words. Rotate between these hook families:
- **The shared moment:** "You know that moment when the interviewer says 'any questions for us?' and your brain goes blank?"
- **The confession:** "Okay, confession. I ignored this tool for six months."
- **The real question:** "A friend asked me last week if she should still learn X now that AI writes the code." Only when someone really asked.
- **The scene:** "<a place your viewers know>. Forty minutes. Not moving. Perfect time to read the news that just broke."
- **The small true number:** "Twelve grams. That's how much the thing that changed my evenings weighs." Only real numbers.
- **The imagine:** "Imagine your first manager is an AI agent." Use when you have no personal story for the topic.
- **The cliffhanger list:** "Three things nobody told me before my first code review. The third one still haunts me."
- **The flip (at most once a week):** "Most people think X. But Y."

Never open with "So today...", "Hey guys", a headline read out loud, or a question with an obvious answer.

## Structures for Shorts (45-60 s, about 130-170 words)
Pick one per script and name it in the notes after the script (never inside it). Don't use the same
structure twice in a row, or more than twice in a week.
1. **Story first, news second:** a moment from your life, "and that's why this news made me stop", what happened with hard specifics, what it means for the viewer, a callback to the moment.
2. **The confession:** what you believed or avoided, what changed your mind, what you found, who should care and who can skip it, a light self-own to close.
3. **Your week, rewritten:** a "you" scene from the viewer's ordinary day, the news walks into it, how the day changes, one thing to try this week.
4. **The experiment diary:** "I gave myself one week to...", Day 1 / Day 3 / Day 7, the surprise, the verdict. Only for things you really did.
5. **The argument:** "the argument everyone in <your field> is having this week", both sides in turn, the facts settle it, your honest lean.
6. **The local analogy:** the news explained as something from your city or everyday life, mapped to the facts, then where the analogy breaks (the real insight).
7. **The note to a junior:** "If you're starting your first job this year, here's what this means for you", two or three short lessons, a warm sign-off.
8. **The slow reveal:** open on a strange detail without explaining it, hold it, reveal why, then what it means.
9. **Countdown with a twist:** a short list whose last item is the surprising or personal one.
10. **The myth flip:** "Most people think X. But Y." → "So what actually happened?" → specifics → your professional lens → so what, now → a measured take.

## Narration craft
- Specific beats general: "23 tabs", not "lots of tabs"; the street name, not "traffic".
- Show the moment first, then say what it means.
- Open one loop early and close it near the end.
- Talk to one person, never "guys".
- A one-line paragraph is a pause. Use one or two per Short.
- Your filler words: <"honestly", "actually", "so", "okay">. Keep them, but sparingly.

## Humour: a pinch, not a sprinkle
One small light beat per Short, two or three in a long-form. It makes the point land; it never replaces it.
Self-aware and dry, mostly at your own expense or about everyday life in your field. Never in the first
line, never at a viewer's, guest's, company's or group's expense, never blurring a number or a fact.

## Lifestyle vlog voice
Narrated, slower and warmer, present-tense voice-over with small time stamps ("7:40 AM. Coffee first,
email second."). Show the honest bits too. Each vlog still leaves one useful takeaway.
Formats: a day in your job; one gadget or app across a real week; desk setup or what's in my bag; a
no-tech day; a work moment (interview day, launch week) told as a story; an event in your city; what the
lifestyle costs a month. Never film inside your employer's offices or show anything internal.

## Long-form
Open with a 20-30 second story, then the promise. Each chapter starts with its own small moment. Re-hook
every 60-90 seconds. End on a callback to the opening story.
